One fixed dual-band filter.
Separate Ha and OIII focus positions.

VFS uses a Raspberry Pi Pico to present three virtual filter slots to ASIAIR. This lets ASIAIR apply user-calibrated ZWO EAF offsets while the physical dual-band filter remains permanently installed.

The observation

One autofocus position may not be optimal for both channels.

In the tested optical setup, the Ha- and OIII-dominated channels reached their best-focus positions at different ZWO EAF coordinates.

This effect is not limited to one setup. In fast imaging systems, the shallow critical-focus zone makes small wavelength-dependent focus shifts easier to see. Residual longitudinal chromatic aberration from refractive elements in the telescope, reducer or corrector—and other wavelength-dependent effects across the complete optical train—can place the red and green parts of a dual-band image at slightly different best-focus positions.

Bahtinov mask observation

The two color channels align at different focuser positions.

Two 3-second exposures were taken 140 ZWO EAF steps apart. The Bahtinov pattern shifts from alignment in the green channel to alignment in the red channel.

The 140-step change moves the middle Bahtinov spike from the centered position in one color channel to the centered position in the other. These screenshots illustrate the observed focus separation in this setup; they are not a universal offset or a precision calibration.

Observation only: use repeated measurements in your own optical system to determine reliable channel offsets.
The size and cause of any focus separation depend on the complete optical system. VFS does not assume a universal offset.

How it works

The emulator changes the virtual slot. ASIAIR moves the focuser.

ASIAIR selects a virtual slot and applies the corresponding offset through the real ZWO EAF.

Imaging plan

Begin and end each target block on the reference slot.

Reference → Ha exposures → OIII exposures → Reference

Start with a reference exposure, place the Ha and OIII imaging between the reference entries, then return to Reference so the next autofocus run begins from the expected virtual slot.

Detailed plan example

Shorter repeated target blocks determine how often autofocus runs.

  • Plan meridian flips between blocks where practical.
  • Allow time for slewing, settling, dithering, downloads, and autofocus.

Scope and limitations

A deliberately narrow interoperability experiment.

VFS does

  • Emulate the tested USB HID filter-wheel behaviour.
  • Expose three virtual slots to ASIAIR.
  • Let ASIAIR activate user-measured offsets.

VFS does not

  • Move or replace the physical filter.
  • Control the ZWO EAF directly.
  • Measure or calculate offsets.
  • Improve optics by itself.
  • Guarantee future ASIAIR compatibility.
Independent open-source interoperability experiment. Not an official ZWO product and not affiliated with or endorsed by ZWO.
